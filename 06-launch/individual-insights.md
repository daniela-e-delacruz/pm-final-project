> _The personal reflection that closes your final project._

## Friction points

The hardest part was M1, which I iterated three times. I started from my own interpretation of the brief, but as I moved through the modules, the qualitative research didn't cover my case, even though I still think it was valid. Then the quantitative data didn't support the second case I identified, so I had to decide what was more representative: the qualitative or the quantitative analysis. Since what I needed to understand was behavior, and behavior is clearer in quantitative metrics, I pivoted again to align my persona, case and problem with the data. I made it explicit that the qualitative research didn't support this and that new interviews are needed, because coordinators may see their workaround as a good process, and people don't complain about a process they think works.

## Key learnings

I realized that in my current role I already do the work of a PM, but without technique or documentation. That's why I decided to take this course, and it confirmed it. From now on, I would start every initiative with a clear definition of the problem and the value proposition, like in M1, because it's the base for getting a valid result. I also learned how to run experiments with structure. In my company we do A/B testing, but without clear definitions, only with intuition: we don't decide the metric, the minimum lift or the decision rules before seeing the results. Other things I'll take with me are building a PRD that brings all the information together, and analyzing data by combining qualitative and quantitative sources, knowing they can tell different stories and that the decision depends on what I need to understand.

## Aha! moment

Honestly, my aha moment was realizing that I already knew what I was doing, just in a different way. That unblocked me, and I stopped pivoting and moved forward with more confidence. I explained why I made each decision, and from that moment the work flowed. I was frozen and overwhelmed by the data until I started making decisions and saw it clearly. Once I understood the material, I got over feeling intimidated by the unknown and learned to trust my own judgment.
